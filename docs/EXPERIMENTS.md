# Experiments

All numbers on this page are copied by a script from the run files in `eval_results/` (tables are
generated by `python -m specbridge.eval.experiments --split dev|test --render`); each row carries
its run id. Nothing here was typed by hand except the interpretation text.

## Setup and decision rules

- **Corpus:** 51 synthetic datasheets + 9 real datasheets ingested into the same index (the real ones
  act as distractors; no retrieval question targets them). Embeddings: `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`.
- **Retrieval questions:** QA items that have expected sources (unanswerable items excluded):
  **dev n=56, test n=37**. A *hit* is a retrieved chunk from an expected `doc_key` **and** an expected page.
- **Cost:** retrieval experiments make no LLM calls (`provider=fake`, cost $0).
- **Protocol (set before looking at test):** configurations are chosen on **dev**. A difference is only
  called an improvement if the two 95% Wilson intervals of recall@5 are disjoint (`significant`);
  `weak` = partial overlap, `within noise` = at least half of the narrower interval overlaps.
  In the "within noise" case the default is left unchanged (or the simpler/cheaper option is
  preferred). **Test** is then run once for confirmation; it is not used to tune.
- MRR is a mean of reciprocal ranks, so it has no Wilson interval; its delta is shown without a noise flag.
- **Small samples:** with n=56 / n=37 the intervals are wide (recall@5 of 0.91 has a 95% CI of
  roughly 0.81-0.96). Treat anything but large effects as unresolved.

## Experiment 1 - chunking (`fixed` vs `layout`, and a control)

**Hypothesis:** layout-aware chunks (headings, tables as their own chunk, context header) retrieve
better than fixed 300-token windows. **Control added during the experiment:** the first comparison
is confounded because layout chunks carry a context header and fixed chunks do not, so a third
index (`fixed` + header) was built to separate the two effects.

Dev:

**Experiment 1 - chunking** (dev split)

| config | run id | R@1 | R@3 | R@5 [95% CI] | R@10 | MRR | R@5 vs ref | MRR vs ref | p50 ms | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exp1-chunking-layout | `20261118-134610_ba6fde4_exp1-chunking-layout` | 0.554 | 0.804 | 0.911 [0.81, 0.96] | 0.929 | 0.684 | (reference) | (reference) | 25 | 56 |
| exp1-chunking-fixed | `20261118-134614_ba6fde4_exp1-chunking-fixed` | 0.393 | 0.554 | 0.643 [0.51, 0.76] | 0.786 | 0.502 | significant | -0.182 (no CI) | 25 | 56 |
| exp1-chunking-fixed-header | `20261118-135103_ba6fde4_exp1-chunking-fixed-header` | 0.607 | 0.732 | 0.857 [0.74, 0.93] | 0.946 | 0.704 | within noise | +0.019 (no CI) | 22 | 56 |

Test (confirmation):

**Experiment 1 - chunking** (test split)

| config | run id | R@1 | R@3 | R@5 [95% CI] | R@10 | MRR | R@5 vs ref | MRR vs ref | p50 ms | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exp1-chunking-layout | `20261118-134853_ba6fde4_exp1-chunking-layout` | 0.486 | 0.730 | 0.865 [0.72, 0.94] | 0.919 | 0.635 | (reference) | (reference) | 25 | 37 |
| exp1-chunking-fixed | `20261118-134857_ba6fde4_exp1-chunking-fixed` | 0.432 | 0.541 | 0.676 [0.51, 0.80] | 0.838 | 0.536 | weak | -0.099 (no CI) | 24 | 37 |
| exp1-chunking-fixed-header | `20261118-135105_ba6fde4_exp1-chunking-fixed-header` | 0.595 | 0.811 | 0.865 [0.72, 0.94] | 0.946 | 0.707 | within noise | +0.073 (no CI) | 20 | 37 |

**Interpretation.** Layout chunks beat plain fixed chunks by a large, significant margin on dev
(recall@5 0.911 vs 0.643). But fixed chunks **with the context header** are statistically
indistinguishable from layout chunks on both splits (dev 0.857 vs 0.911, test 0.865 vs 0.865).
So on this corpus the gain comes from the **context header**, not from the layout-aware chunk
boundaries. **Decision:** keep `CHUNK_STRATEGY=layout` (no measured disadvantage; table chunks keep
their header row and give tight citation highlights, which fixed windows cannot), but do not claim
that layout chunking improves retrieval; the evidence only supports the header.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exp2-mode-vector | `20261118-134858_ba6fde4_exp2-mode-vector` | 0.757 | 0.865 | 0.892 [0.75, 0.96] | 0.946 | 0.815 | within noise | +0.181 (no CI) | 22 | 37 |
| exp2-mode-keyword | `20261118-134859_ba6fde4_exp2-mode-keyword` | 0.216 | 0.351 | 0.514 [0.36, 0.67] | 0.703 | 0.336 | significant | -0.299 (no CI) | 23 | 37 |
| exp2-mode-hybrid | `20261118-134900_ba6fde4_exp2-mode-hybrid` | 0.486 | 0.730 | 0.865 [0.72, 0.94] | 0.919 | 0.635 | (reference) | (reference) | 26 | 37 |

**Interpretation.** Keyword-only search is significantly worse than either alternative (its OR-query
of content words is a weak ranker). **Hybrid did not beat vector-only**: on dev they are within
noise (recall@5 0.911 vs 0.875), and vector-only is numerically equal or better on most other metrics
on both splits (e.g. test recall@1 0.757 vs 0.486). Fusing in a weak keyword ranking appears to
dilute the top ranks. **Decision:** `RETRIEVAL_MODE=hybrid` stays the default because dev shows no
significant difference and keyword matching is a safeguard for exact part numbers that this
embedding-friendly synthetic corpus may not stress; this is a judgement call, not a measured win,
and `vector` is a defensible alternative. Revisit with real datasheets.

## Experiment 3 - cross-encoder reranker

**Hypothesis:** reranking the fused top 20 with `BAAI/bge-reranker-base` improves precision at the
cost of latency.

Dev:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exp2-mode-hybrid | `20261118-134618_ba6fde4_exp2-mode-hybrid` | 0.554 | 0.804 | 0.911 [0.81, 0.96] | 0.929 | 0.684 | (reference) | (reference) | 26 | 56 |
| exp3-hybrid-rerank | `20261118-134750_ba6fde4_exp3-hybrid-rerank` | 0.875 | 0.946 | 0.946 [0.85, 0.98] | 0.946 | 0.908 | within noise | +0.223 (no CI) | 810 | 56 |
| exp3-hybrid-rerank-v1-content-only | `20261118-134620_ba6fde4_exp3-hybrid-rerank` | 0.339 | 0.464 | 0.589 [0.46, 0.71] | 0.732 | 0.440 | significant | -0.245 (no CI) | 788 | 56 |

Test (confirmation):

**Experiment 3 - reranker** (test split)

| config | run id | R@1 | R@3 | R@5 [95% CI] | R@10 | MRR | R@5 vs ref | MRR vs ref | p50 ms | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exp2-mode-hybrid | `20261118-134900_ba6fde4_exp2-mode-hybrid` | 0.486 | 0.730 | 0.865 [0.72, 0.94] | 0.919 | 0.635 | (reference) | (reference) | 26 | 37 |
| exp3-hybrid-rerank | `20261118-134901_ba6fde4_exp3-hybrid-rerank` | 0.811 | 0.919 | 0.919 [0.79, 0.97] | 0.946 | 0.868 | within noise | +0.233 (no CI) | 805 | 37 |

**Interpretation.** The first implementation (`exp3-hybrid-rerank-v1-content-only`) made retrieval
**much worse** (recall@5 0.589): the reranker was scoring the bare chunk content, and a table chunk
on its own does not say which part it describes (that lives in the context header). After changing
the reranker input to *context header + content* (the same text the embedder sees; regression test
`test_reranker_sees_context_header_and_content`) it improves the top ranks substantially
(dev recall@1 0.875 vs 0.554, MRR 0.908 vs 0.684) while recall@5 is within noise (0.946 vs 0.911).
It costs about 800 ms per query on CPU versus about 25 ms. **Decision:** `RERANK_ENABLED=false` by
default (the QA prompt receives 8 chunks, where recall@5 is what matters and the gain is within
noise); enable it when the single best chunk matters more than latency.

## Experiment 7 - `ABSTAIN_MIN_SCORE` calibration

**Question:** can the best vector similarity of the retrieved chunks be used to abstain *before*
calling the LLM? The sweep treats "abstain iff best similarity < t".

Dev (where the threshold would be chosen):

**Experiment 7 - abstention threshold** (dev split, run `20261118-134707_ba6fde4_exp7-abstain-calibration`)

mean top vector similarity: answerable 0.706 (n=56), unanswerable 0.672 (n=9)

| threshold | abstained | abstention precision | abstention recall | answerable questions blocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.55 | 0 | 0/0 | 0/9 | 0/56 |
| 0.60 | 2 | 2/2 | 2/9 | 0/56 |
| 0.62 | 4 | 2/4 | 2/9 | 2/56 |
| 0.65 | 7 | 2/7 | 2/9 | 5/56 |
| 0.70 | 30 | 5/30 | 5/9 | 25/56 |
| 0.75 | 54 | 8/54 | 8/9 | 46/56 |

Test (confirmation):

**Experiment 7 - abstention threshold** (test split, run `20261118-134931_ba6fde4_exp7-abstain-calibration`)

mean top vector similarity: answerable 0.716 (n=37), unanswerable 0.675 (n=6)

| threshold | abstained | abstention precision | abstention recall | answerable questions blocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.55 | 0 | 0/0 | 0/6 | 0/37 |
| 0.60 | 1 | 0/1 | 0/6 | 1/37 |
| 0.62 | 1 | 0/1 | 0/6 | 1/37 |
| 0.65 | 7 | 2/7 | 2/6 | 5/37 |
| 0.70 | 20 | 5/20 | 5/6 | 15/37 |
| 0.75 | 32 | 6/32 | 6/6 | 26/37 |

**Interpretation.** The retrieval score barely separates answerable from unanswerable questions
(mean similarity 0.706 vs 0.672 on dev). Questions about a missing attribute of a real part, or a
non-existent part number, still retrieve plausible chunks. On dev the stated criterion (largest
threshold with at most 5% of answerable questions blocked) gives **0.62**, which catches 2 of 9
unanswerable questions; **on test the same threshold blocks 1 of 37 answerable questions and 0 of 6
unanswerable ones**, so it does not transfer. **Decision:** `ABSTAIN_MIN_SCORE=0.0` (retrieval-score
abstention disabled). Abstention relies on the model's `answerable` flag and on citation
verification, which are LLM-dependent and therefore **not yet measured against a real model**
(blocked: needs API key); the fake-provider QA runs are a pipeline check only.

## Summary of decisions

| setting | value | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `CHUNK_STRATEGY` | `layout` | no measured disadvantage; header, not layout, explains the retrieval gain (exp 1) |
| context header | on | significant, large effect on dev and test (exp 4) |
| `RETRIEVAL_MODE` | `hybrid` | within noise of `vector` on dev; kept as a judgement call (exp 2) |
| `RERANK_ENABLED` | `false` | +top-rank quality but ~30x latency; recall@5 within noise (exp 3) |
| `ABSTAIN_MIN_SCORE` | `0.0` | no threshold generalises from dev to test (exp 7) |
| extraction model / retry | unchanged defaults | blocked: needs API key (exp 5, 6) |

The final baseline and test-split report are listed in the "Final baseline" section of
[EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md).
